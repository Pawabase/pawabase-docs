**Deliverability** is whether your email reaches the inbox, not just whether it was sent. Pawabase hands each message to your provider and records that the provider **accepted** it. Whether Gmail, Outlook or Apple then puts it in the inbox, in spam, or rejects it depends mostly on your **domain's reputation and set-up**. This page is the checklist.

## 1. Send from your own domain

Send from an address on a domain you own (`orders@northwind.com`), not a free mailbox (`northwind@gmail.com`). Major mailbox providers reject or spam-folder mail that claims to be from a free mailbox but isn't sent by it.

Many teams use a **subdomain** for app mail, such as `mail.northwind.com` or `notify.northwind.com`. Its reputation is then separate from your people's everyday email.

## 2. Verify the domain with your provider

Every provider asks you to prove you own the domain by adding DNS records it gives you. Do this first; until it's done most providers refuse to send, and the message shows as **failed** in [Mail activity](/mail/activity) with the provider's reason.

## 3. SPF, DKIM and DMARC

These three DNS records tell receiving servers your mail is genuine. Gmail and Yahoo require them for anyone sending in volume.

| Record | What it proves | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SPF** | Your provider's servers may send for your domain | A `TXT` record your provider gives you, such as `v=spf1 include:… ~all` |
| **DKIM** | The message was signed by your domain and not changed on the way | One or more records (`TXT` or `CNAME`) your provider gives you |
| **DMARC** | What receivers should do with mail that fails SPF or DKIM, and where to send reports | A `TXT` record at `_dmarc.yourdomain.com` that you write |

A sensible DMARC to start with, which only reports and doesn't block anything:

```text
_dmarc.northwind.com  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@northwind.com"
```

Once reports show your real mail passing, tighten it to `p=quarantine`, then `p=reject`, so others can't send as you.

<Note>
A domain can have only **one** SPF record. If you send through more than one service (your provider, your company mail), merge them into one record with several `include:` parts.
</Note>

## 4. Separate kinds of mail

Keep **transactional** mail (receipts, resets, codes, which people expect) apart from **marketing** mail (newsletters, announcements, which some people mark as spam).

- Use different [senders](/mail/delivery#senders), such as `orders@` and `news@`.
- Ideally send them through different subdomains or provider streams, so a marketing complaint never delays a password reset.
- Use template [categories](/mail/templates#details) to keep them organised.

## 5. Write mail filters trust

The template [checks](/mail/templates#check-preview-and-test) help with most of this:

- Always have a **subject** and **preview text**.
- Include a **plain-text** version.
- Don't make the email **mostly images**. Spam filters weigh the text-to-image ratio, and many people read with images off.
- Keep the HTML under **102 KB**, or Gmail cuts it behind *View entire message*.
- Use **https** links and images.
- Make link text say where it goes, not "click here".
- Include your **postal address** and, for marketing, a way to unsubscribe.

## 6. Watch bounces and complaints

Your provider sees what happens **after** it accepts a message: hard bounces (the address doesn't exist), soft bounces (a full mailbox) and spam complaints. Pawabase shows what the server answered when it took the message, not these later events.

- Check your provider's dashboard regularly, and after each campaign.
- Stop sending to addresses that hard-bounce. Many providers do this automatically with a suppression list.
- Keep complaints very low. Above about 0.3% of messages, mailbox providers start filtering you.
- Only email people who signed up or have an account. Never buy lists.

## 7. Grow volume gradually

A new domain has no reputation. If you'll send a lot, start with your most engaged people and increase over a few weeks. Sudden spikes from a new domain look like spam.

## 8. Test before you launch

1. Send a test of each important template to a Gmail, an Outlook and an Apple address.
2. Check the inbox **and** the spam folder.
3. In Gmail, open the message and **Show original**: SPF, DKIM and DMARC should all say **PASS**.
4. Fix anything that fails before launch, not after.

## Checklist

- [ ] Sending from your own domain (or subdomain)
- [ ] Domain verified with your provider
- [ ] SPF, DKIM and DMARC records added, and passing in **Show original**
- [ ] Separate senders for transactional and marketing mail
- [ ] Plain-text versions, preview text, https links, postal address
- [ ] Provider dashboard bookmarked for bounces and complaints
- [ ] An [alarm](/activity/alarms) on mail failures
