A company login does more than authenticate. It decides who is in the customer's organization and with which role, so the company's own directory stays the place where access is managed.

## Just-in-time membership

Nobody needs an invitation. At each sign-in through a connection:

| The person | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Has no account | An account is created with the provider's address (confirmed, when the [address is trusted](/auth/enterprise-connections#trust-the-email-address)), name and avatar. This works even when [public sign-up](/auth/sign-up-restrictions) is off |
| Has an account with the same address | The company identity is linked to it, when the address is trusted. The person keeps their data, other sign-in methods and memberships |
| Is not a member of the organization | They become one, with the role below. The organization's activity shows `member.joined` with `via: "sso"`, and `organization.member_joined` is emitted |
| Is already a member | Their role is left alone, unless a group mapping says otherwise |

The membership takes a seat. When the organization's [seats](/auth/organizations#plans-seats-and-suspension-from-your-server) are full, the sign-in fails with `organization_full` and nobody is added.

Removing someone from the organization does not stop them signing in: they rejoin at their next company sign-in. To take someone's access away, remove them in the company's directory (or unassign them from the app there). To keep a person out of your whole app, [disable their account](/auth/users).

## The role for new members

**Role for new members** (`default_role`) is the role people join with when no group mapping applies. It starts as `member`. Any role the organization has can be used, including [roles of its own](/auth/organizations#roles-of-the-organizations-own), except `owner`: a company login never makes owners. Make the customer's first administrator an owner yourself, or have them create the organization and [transfer ownership](/auth/organizations#transfer-ownership).

<CodeGroup>
```typescript TypeScript
await a.ssoConnections.update("acme-okta", { defaultRole: "viewer" });
```

```python Python
a.sso_connections.update("acme-okta", default_role="viewer")
```
</CodeGroup>

## Roles from the company's groups

Companies already group their people: *Engineering*, *Finance*, *App Admins*. Map those groups to the organization's roles and the company manages access in its own directory.

1. Ask the company's administrator to send groups in the sign-in: a **groups claim** in OpenID Connect (Okta, Entra ID, Keycloak; Google Workspace cannot), a **groups attribute** in SAML.
2. On the connection's **Roles** tab, enter the claim or attribute name in **Groups attribute**, usually `groups` (SAML providers also use `memberOf` or a claim URI such as `http://schemas.microsoft.com/ws/2008/06/identity/claims/groups`).
3. Under **Groups that give a role**, pair group names with roles.

<CodeGroup>
```typescript TypeScript
await a.ssoConnections.update("acme-okta", {
  groupsAttribute: "groups",
  roleMapping: { "App Admins": "admin", "Finance": "billing", "Contractors": "viewer" },
});
```

```python Python
a.sso_connections.update(
    "acme-okta",
    groups_attribute="groups",
    role_mapping={"App Admins": "admin", "Finance": "billing", "Contractors": "viewer"},
)
```
</CodeGroup>

How the mapping is applied:

- **At every sign-in**, not only the first. Move someone to *App Admins* in the directory and they are an admin the next time they sign in; take them out of every mapped group and they keep their current role until a mapped group says otherwise.
- **The first match wins**, in the order the mapping is listed. Put the most powerful role first.
- Group names match **without regard to case**.
- A group that maps to a role the organization no longer has is ignored (a newcomer then gets the role for new members).
- An **owner** is never demoted by a mapping.
- Each change is recorded in the organization's activity as `member.role_changed` with `via: "sso"`, the role before and after.

Entra ID sends group **object IDs** (`a1b2c3…`) unless the app is set to send names; map the IDs. A person in many groups may receive a *group overage* claim instead of the list; ask the administrator to send only the groups assigned to the app.

## Require the company login

Once everyone at the company has signed in through the connection, turn on **Require it** (`required`). From then on, for anyone whose address is at one of the organization's verified domains:

| Way in | With the company login required |
| --- | --- |
| The company login | Works |
| Password sign-in | Refused before the password is checked: `403` `sso_required` |
| Sign-up with a password | Refused: `403` `sso_required` |
| [Email link](/auth/sign-in-links), [email code](/auth/email-codes), [passkey](/auth/passkeys), [social sign-in](/auth/social-sign-in), [account recovery](/auth/account-recovery) | The session is refused: `403` `sso_required` |
| A session that already exists | Keeps working until it ends or is [revoked](/auth/sessions) |

The refusal names the connection:

```json
{
  "code": "sso_required",
  "message": "Acme signs its people in with its company login",
  "connection": "acme-okta",
  "organization": "acme"
}
```

See [When the company login is required](/auth/enterprise-sign-in#when-the-company-login-is-required) for handling it in your sign-in form.

Things to know before switching it on:

- It applies to **everyone at the domains**, members or not, including people who signed up before the company had a connection.
- To end the other sessions at once, revoke them: **Users & auth → Protection → Sessions**, or `admin.users.revokeSessions(userId)` for each person.
- Requiring needs the connection on. Switching the connection off also turns **Require it** off, so nobody is locked out by a connection that cannot be used: they fall back to the other sign-in methods.
- If the organization is suspended or loses its last verified domain, the connection stops routing and requiring.
- Keep a way in for yourself: your own staff should not use an address at the customer's domain.

<CodeGroup>
```typescript TypeScript
await a.ssoConnections.update("acme-okta", { required: true });
```

```python Python
a.sso_connections.update("acme-okta", required=True)
```
</CodeGroup>

## In your policies and data

A session from a company login is scoped to the organization, like a sign-in that chose the organization:

| Claim | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `org` | The organization's slug, `acme` |
| `org_role` | The person's role in it |
| `org_perms` | What that role allows |
| `org_id`, `org_teams` | The organization's id and the person's teams in it |

Your [organization rules](/auth/organizations#rules-for-organizations) and per-organization data work unchanged. Sign-in activity records the method as `sso:<connection>`, so a rule or a report can tell a company sign-in from a password.

## A typical rollout

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify the domain">
    The customer's admin claims `acme.com` on the organization and publishes the DNS record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect, test, switch on">
    Create the connection, exchange details, **Test connection**, and turn it on. People can now use either their password or the company login.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Map groups">
    Agree with the customer which of their groups become admins, and map them. Everyone else joins with the role for new members.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Announce, then require">
    Tell the customer's people to use **Sign in with your company**. After a week or two, turn on **Require it**, and revoke the remaining password sessions.
  </Step>
</Steps>
