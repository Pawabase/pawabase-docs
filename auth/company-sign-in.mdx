Business customers want their staff to sign in with the company login they already have (Okta, Microsoft Entra, Google Workspace) instead of another password. Pawabase calls this **company sign-in**, and supports the two standards behind it: **SAML** and **OpenID Connect (OIDC)**.

You create one **connection** per customer company. Each connection says which identity provider it uses and which email domains it covers.

## In Studio

**Users & auth → Sign-in → Company sign-in.** The empty page says *Connect your customers' identity providers*. Choose **Add a company connection**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="How does the company sign in?">
    Choose **SAML** or **OpenID Connect**. Ask your customer's IT team which they use; most support both.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connection details">
    Fill in the fields below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finish setup with the provider">
    The page shows the values your customer must enter on their side. Send them across and ask the customer to test.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Connection details (both types)

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Connection name** | A label for you, such as `acme-entra` |
| **Company email domains** | Press Enter after each. When set, the connection accepts only email addresses at these domains (for example `acme.com`) |
| **Enable this connection** | Allow users to sign in with it once the provider details are complete |
| **Trust the email address** | Treat the email asserted by this identity provider as verified |

### SAML

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Identity provider entity ID** | The provider's unique id, such as `https://idp.acme.com/metadata` |
| **Single sign-on URL** | Where Pawabase sends people to sign in, such as `https://idp.acme.com/sso` |
| **Signing certificate** | The provider's public PEM certificate. Pawabase verifies SAML responses against it |

**Attribute mapping** says which SAML attribute fills which Pawabase field: a table of *Pawabase field* (`name`, `email`…) and *SAML attribute* (`displayName`…).

Give your customer these values to enter in their provider (shown on the **Finish setup** step): **Entity ID**, **Assertion consumer service URL** and **Metadata URL**.

### OpenID Connect

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Issuer** | The provider's address, such as `https://login.acme.com`. Pawabase discovers the provider's endpoints from its OpenID configuration |
| **Client ID** | From the provider |
| **Client secret** | From the provider. Use `secret://NAME` to read it from **Secrets** |

Give your customer the **Redirect URI** shown on the finish step to register with their provider.

## What people experience

1. Someone types their work email on your sign-in page.
2. Pawabase matches the domain to a connection and sends them to their company login.
3. They sign in there (with their company's own rules, including two-step).
4. They return to your app, signed in. The first time, an account is created; the identity appears under their [linked accounts](/auth/linked-accounts).

Your sign-in screen should offer **"Sign in with your company"** and take an email. Start the redirect for that connection the same way as a [social sign-in](/auth/social-sign-in), using the connection's name as the provider:

```typescript
pawabase.auth.signInWithOAuth("acme-entra", { redirectTo: "https://myapp.com/" });
```

## Customer onboarding checklist

1. Agree the protocol (SAML or OIDC) and the email domains.
2. Create the connection; keep the secret in **Secrets**.
3. Send the customer your **Entity ID / ACS URL** (SAML) or **Redirect URI** (OIDC).
4. Receive their entity ID, SSO URL and certificate (SAML), or issuer, client ID and secret (OIDC).
5. Enter them, tick **Enable this connection**, and have the customer test with a real user.
6. Optionally turn off [sign-ups](/auth/sign-up-restrictions) for that customer's domain so only company sign-in works.

## Good to know

- Domain matching decides which connection applies, so use each company's exact domains.
- **Trust the email address** matters if your rules need verified emails; only tick it for providers you trust to verify addresses.
- Certificates expire. Note the date and rotate it with the customer before then.
- Two-step verification for these users is usually enforced by the company's provider; you can still require your own.
