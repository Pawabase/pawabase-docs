Before a product can show **Sign in with Pawabase**, it needs to be registered. Registration gives it a **client id** (public, sent in the browser) and a **client secret** (private, kept on your server), and tells Pawabase where it may send people back to.

Apps belong to an organization: the one that builds the product. Register it in your company's own Pawabase organization, not in a customer's. Your customers connect their organizations to it later, through the consent screen.

## Who can register apps

Owners and admins of the organization can register, edit, rotate and delete apps. Members and viewers can see the list (**Settings → Integrations → Your apps**) but not change it.

## Register

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Integrations">
    In the dashboard, open your organization, then **Settings → Integrations**. The page has three parts: **Connected apps**, **Your apps** and **Personal access tokens**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register app">
    In **Your apps**, click **Register app**. A form opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the form">
    Give it a name, choose its type, add at least one callback URL and choose the scopes it may ask for. The fields are described below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the secret">
    After **Register app**, Pawabase shows the client id and the **client secret** once. Copy the secret into your server's secret store now. Pawabase keeps only a hash of it and can't show it again; if it is lost, make a new one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The fields

| Field | Required | Shown to people | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Name** | Yes | Yes: "**\{Name\}** wants to connect to Pawabase" | Up to 80 characters |
| **Description** | No | No (for your team) | Up to 300 characters |
| **Website** | No | Yes: its host, next to "by \{organization\}" | An `http://` or `https://` address |
| **Logo URL** | No | Yes: in the tile on the consent card. Without one, the first letter of the name is shown | An `http://` or `https://` address to a square image. It is loaded by the person's browser |
| **Type** | Yes (chosen once) | No | **OAuth app** or **Platform app**. Can't be changed later |
| **Callback URLs** | Yes for an OAuth app | The host of the one used, under the buttons | Up to 10, see below |
| **Scopes it may ask for** | Yes | The ones it asks for, as plain sentences | Any of the five [scopes](/integrations/scopes-and-roles). All five are on by default |

The consent screen also shows **by \{organization name\}**: the name of the organization that registered the app. People use it to judge whether to trust the request, so register from an organization whose name they will recognise.

## OAuth app or platform app

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="OAuth app">
    For products that many Pawabase customers use. Each person connects their own organization through the consent screen, and the product acts as that person.

    - Gets tokens through the **authorization code** flow (with PKCE).
    - Needs at least one callback URL.
    - Can't use `client_credentials`: asking for it is answered `unauthorized_client`.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Platform app">
    For products that also make Pawabase projects themselves, in their own organization, and later hand them to their users.

    - Can do everything an OAuth app can.
    - Can also get a token with **client credentials** (client id + secret, no person). That token acts as an admin of the organization that registered the app.
    - Can create **claim links**: one-time links a user opens to move a project into an organization of their own.
    - Callback URLs are optional (needed only if it also uses the consent flow).

    See [Platform apps and claim links](/integrations/platform-apps).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Callback URLs

The callback URL is where Pawabase sends the browser after the person decides. Pawabase only sends people to a URL you registered, so a stolen client id can't be used to send codes somewhere else.

The rules:

| Kind | Example | Allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HTTPS | `https://app.example.com/oauth/pawabase/callback` | Yes |
| HTTP on the loopback | `http://127.0.0.1:4400/callback`, `http://localhost:3000/cb`, `http://[::1]:8080/cb` | Yes: for local development, desktop apps and CLIs |
| HTTP anywhere else | `http://app.example.com/callback` | No: refused with "use https (http is only for 127.0.0.1 or localhost)" |
| A custom scheme | `acme-ide://oauth/callback` | Yes: for desktop and mobile apps that register a scheme |
| `javascript:`, `data:`, `file:`, `vbscript:` | | No |
| With a `#fragment` | `https://app.example.com/cb#x` | No |

**Matching is exact.** The `redirect_uri` your product sends must match a registered one character for character: scheme, host, port, path and query. `https://app.example.com/cb` and `https://app.example.com/cb/` are different URLs.

**One exception: loopback ports.** Desktop apps and CLIs usually listen on whatever port is free. If you register `http://127.0.0.1:4400/callback`, Pawabase also accepts `http://127.0.0.1:52817/callback`: same scheme, host and path, any port. This follows RFC 8252 (OAuth for native apps). It applies only to `http` on `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` and `[::1]`.

**Leaving `redirect_uri` out.** If an app has exactly one callback URL, `/oauth/authorize` uses it when the request has no `redirect_uri`. With two or more, the request must say which one. Sending it explicitly is always clearer.

<Tip>
Register one URL per environment of your product: `https://app.example.com/oauth/pawabase/callback` for production, `https://staging.example.com/oauth/pawabase/callback` for staging, and `http://127.0.0.1:3000/oauth/pawabase/callback` for local development. Or register separate apps per environment so a staging secret can't be used in production.
</Tip>

## Scopes an app may ask for

The **Scopes it may ask for** switches set an upper limit. A request to `/oauth/authorize` (or `client_credentials`) asking for a scope outside this list is refused with `invalid_scope`.

Keep the list as small as your product needs. A product that only reads what's in projects doesn't need `project:write`; one that never creates projects doesn't need `projects:write`. People are more likely to approve a short list, and a smaller list limits the damage if your secret leaks.

What each scope allows and which role it needs: [Scopes and roles](/integrations/scopes-and-roles).

## What you get

After registering, the app's row in **Your apps** shows:

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **App** | Name, a **platform** tag for platform apps, and the **client id** with a copy button |
| **Secret** | `pbs_…` followed by the last six characters, to tell secrets apart. Never the secret itself |
| **Connections** | How many organizations are connected to it right now |

The client id looks like `pbc_YnlsTGxjACaabxvklZNh`. The secret looks like `pbs_` followed by 43 random characters. Both are opaque strings: don't parse them.

Under the table, the card lists the four addresses your product needs, already filled in with this Pawabase's host.

### What the client id and secret are for

The pair is your product's **client**: how Pawabase recognises *your product* (not any one customer) when it calls `/oauth/*`. The client id is public: it travels in the address people are sent to. The secret is private and stays on your server. One client serves every customer, and each customer who connects gets their own connection and tokens under it. The full picture, including public clients that use no secret at all, is in [Apps, clients and connections](/integrations/clients).

Those two uses are all a client does. In code it is just two values you read from the environment; the SDK wraps them in one object, made once at startup:

<CodeGroup>
```bash cURL
# The client id and secret are used in exactly two places.
export PAWABASE_CLIENT_ID=pbc_YnlsTGxjACaabxvklZNh
export PAWABASE_CLIENT_SECRET=pbs_…

# 1. The client id goes in the address you send people to (it is public).
#    https://dashboard.pawabase.com/oauth/authorize?client_id=$PAWABASE_CLIENT_ID&redirect_uri=…&response_type=code&…

# 2. The id and secret go to /oauth/token and /oauth/revoke, from your server, as HTTP Basic.
curl -s https://dashboard.pawabase.com/oauth/token \
  -u "$PAWABASE_CLIENT_ID:$PAWABASE_CLIENT_SECRET" \
  -d grant_type=refresh_token -d refresh_token=pbr_…
```

```typescript TypeScript (SDK)
import { PawabaseOAuth } from "@pawabase/client/cloud";

const oauth = new PawabaseOAuth({
  clientId: process.env.PAWABASE_CLIENT_ID!,
  clientSecret: process.env.PAWABASE_CLIENT_SECRET!, // leave out for a public client
  redirectUri: "https://app.example.com/oauth/pawabase/callback",
});
```

```python Python (SDK)
from pawabase.cloud import OAuth

oauth = OAuth(
    client_id=os.environ["PAWABASE_CLIENT_ID"],
    client_secret=os.environ["PAWABASE_CLIENT_SECRET"],  # leave out for a public client
    redirect_uri="https://app.example.com/oauth/pawabase/callback",
)
```
</CodeGroup>

## Edit an app

**Edit** on the app's row opens the same form. You can change the name, description, website, logo, callback URLs and scopes. The type can't change.

Changes take effect at once:

- A new **name**, **logo** or **website** shows on the next consent screen. Organizations already connected keep their connection.
- **Removing a callback URL** stops new sign-ins that use it. Tokens already issued keep working.
- **Removing a scope** stops new requests for it. Existing connections keep the scopes they were given until they connect again. To take a scope away from existing connections, delete the app (which disconnects everyone) or ask each organization to disconnect.

## Rotate the secret

**New secret** on the app's row makes a new secret, shows it once, and **stops the old one at once**. There is no overlap period, so:

1. Click **New secret** and copy it.
2. Put it in your server's configuration and deploy.
3. Calls made with the old secret between steps 1 and 2 fail with `invalid_client`.

Rotating doesn't disconnect anyone. Refresh tokens issued before the rotation keep working, as long as your server sends the new secret with them.

Rotate when someone who knew the secret leaves, when it may have been logged or committed, or on a schedule.

## Delete an app

The trash icon deletes the app. This:

- disconnects **every organization** connected to it: all their access and refresh tokens stop working at once,
- deletes any authorization codes not yet used,
- makes `client_credentials` tokens of a platform app stop working,
- is recorded in the organization's activity as `integration.app_deleted`.

It can't be undone. Registering again gives a new client id, and everyone has to connect again.

## Activity

Everything in this page is recorded in **Settings → Activity** of the organization that owns the app:

| Action | When |
| --- | --- |
| `integration.app_created` | An app was registered (with its client id and type) |
| `integration.app_updated` | An app's details changed |
| `integration.secret_rotated` | A new secret was made |
| `integration.app_deleted` | An app was deleted |

Connections made by customers are recorded in **their** organization's activity (`integration.connected`, `integration.disconnected`), not in yours.

## From the command line or code

The dashboard calls these actions, and so can you with a dashboard session (they are not part of the `/v1` Management API, because managing apps is a dashboard task):

| Action | Body |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /rest/v1/x/cloud/integrations.apps.list` | `{}` |
| `POST /rest/v1/x/cloud/integrations.apps.create` | `{"name", "kind"?, "description"?, "website"?, "logo_url"?, "redirect_uris": [...], "scopes"?: [...]}` |
| `POST /rest/v1/x/cloud/integrations.apps.update` | `{"app": "<id or client id>", ...fields to change}` |
| `POST /rest/v1/x/cloud/integrations.apps.rotate_secret` | `{"app": "<id or client id>"}` |
| `POST /rest/v1/x/cloud/integrations.apps.delete` | `{"app": "<id or client id>"}` |

Answers are wrapped in `{"data": ...}`. `create` and `rotate_secret` include `client_secret` once.

## Checklist before going live

- [ ] The app is registered in your company's organization, under a name your users recognise.
- [ ] It has a logo and a website, so the consent card looks trustworthy.
- [ ] Production callback URLs are `https` and match exactly what your server sends.
- [ ] Scopes are the fewest your product needs.
- [ ] The secret is in a secret store, not in code, not in the browser, not in logs.
- [ ] Someone on your team knows how to rotate it.
- [ ] You read [Security](/integrations/security).
