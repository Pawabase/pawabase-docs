This page is for the people who run a Pawabase organization. When someone in your organization clicks **Sign in with Pawabase** in another product and approves it, that product is **connected** to your organization. Here is how to see those connections and end them.

## Where to find them

**Settings → Integrations → Connected apps** lists every product connected to the organization right now:

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **App** | The product's logo and name, and **by** the organization that registered it |
| **Can** | What it was allowed to do, one tag per scope: Organization, Projects: read, Projects: manage, Inside projects: read, Inside projects: change |
| **Connected** | When, and by whom (their email) |
| **Last used** | The last time the product made a request |
| | **Disconnect** (owners and admins) |

Everyone in the organization can see the list. Only owners and admins can disconnect.

## What a connected product can do

Exactly what its tags say, and never more than the role of the person who connected it. The tags mean:

| Tag | Scope | It can |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization | `organizations:read` | See the organization's name, plan and number of projects |
| Projects: read | `projects:read` | See the list of projects and where their APIs are |
| Projects: manage | `projects:write` | Create, rename, pause, restore and **delete** projects, and deploy marketplace apps |
| Inside projects: read | `project:read` | Read everything inside every project: data, users, rules, flows, logs |
| Inside projects: change | `project:write` | Change everything inside every project, as Studio can |

A connection covers **every project in the organization**, including ones created after it was made. There is no per-project connection.

It can never: change billing, invite or remove people, change roles, rename or delete the organization, manage integrations, or move projects to another organization. See [What no scope allows](/tools/integrations/scopes-and-roles#what-no-scope-allows).

## Whose access it uses

A connection acts as the person who approved it, with their role **as it is now**:

- If that person is demoted, the product's power shrinks with them at once.
- If that person leaves the organization or is removed, the product stops working for your organization at once. The connection stays in the list (so you can see it) until it is disconnected or someone connects it again.
- If someone else approves the same product for your organization later, the connection switches to them.

## Disconnect

**Disconnect** on a row, then confirm. At once:

- every access token and refresh token the product holds for your organization stops working,
- its next request is answered `401`,
- the row leaves the list,
- `integration.disconnected` is recorded in **Settings → Activity** with your email.

Disconnecting does **not** undo what the product already did: projects it created stay, data it wrote stays, and **API keys it created in your projects keep working**. Review those after disconnecting:

1. **Projects it created**: **Settings → Activity** shows each `project.created`; the project's record also carries which app made it (`integration` in the Management API).
2. **API keys it created**: open each project's **API keys** page in Studio; keys are named by the product that made them. Revoke the ones you don't want.
3. **What it changed**: the project's server records changes made through the integration under "\{person\} via \{product\}".

To use the product again, someone goes through **Sign in with Pawabase** in it again.

## What you see in Activity

| Action | Recorded when |
| --- | --- |
| `integration.connected` | Someone approved a product (with its client id and scopes) |
| `integration.disconnected` | An admin disconnected it |
| `project.created`, `project.updated`, `project.deleted`, `project.paused`, `project.restored` | A product did it, recorded with the person it acts for |
| `integration.key_created` | A product made an API key in a project |
| `integration.token_created`, `integration.token_revoked` | Someone made or revoked a personal access token |
| `project.transferred_in` | A project was claimed from a platform app into this organization |

## Deciding whether to trust a product

The consent screen and this list show the product's name, logo, website, and **the organization that registered it**. Before approving, or when reviewing the list:

- Is "by …" a company you expect? A product called "Pawabase Helper" registered by an organization you've never heard of deserves suspicion.
- Does it ask for more than it needs? A monitoring tool shouldn't need **Projects: manage**.
- Is it still used? **Last used** months ago means it can probably go.

Pawabase doesn't review or verify third-party products. Treat a connection like giving someone a role in your organization.

## Rules for your organization

A good default policy for organizations with several admins:

- Approve products with write access only after checking with the other admins.
- Prefer products that ask for read-only scopes where that's enough.
- Review **Connected apps** once a quarter, with **Last used**.
- When someone leaves, check which connections were theirs (they've stopped working) and decide whether to reconnect them under someone else.
- Turn on **deletion protection** for production projects: a connected product with **Projects: manage** still can't delete or move them while it's on. Only a person can switch it off.
